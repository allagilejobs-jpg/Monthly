## 1. Educational

**Post type:** Carousel

**Hook/headline:** Autism + ADHD: when support plans miss the second layer

**Key points/script outline:**
- Slide 1: Autism + ADHD can look like "won't listen" when the real issue is competing needs.
- Slide 2: Autism may need predictability, sameness, sensory safety, and processing time.
- Slide 3: ADHD may need novelty, movement, short tasks, immediate feedback, and flexible starts.
- Slide 4: A child can crave routine and still resist starting the routine.
- Slide 5: Try "same order, smaller steps" instead of a brand-new system.
- Slide 6: Ask school and therapists: "Are we supporting attention and regulation, or only compliance?"
- Slide 7: Save this for October ADHD Awareness Month.

## 2. Educational

**Post type:** Reel

**Hook/headline:** The 10-second transition cue that beats repeating yourself

**Key points/script outline:**
- Opening: "If you say 'time to go' twelve times, try changing the cue."
- Show a simple sequence: visual timer, one concrete next step, one body-based cue.
- Script: "When the timer beeps, shoes go on. First shoes, then car."
- Add a regulation cue: hand squeeze, backpack job, wall push, or carry item.
- Explain: transitions are not just listening. They require stopping, shifting, planning, and tolerating uncertainty.
- Close: "Less talking. More predictable cues."

## 7. Trending/Timely

**Post type:** Reel

**Hook/headline:** For ADHD Awareness Month: your autistic child is not "too much"

**Key points/script outline:**
- Opening: "October is ADHD Awareness Month, and a lot of autistic kids are carrying both."
- Beat 1: Big movement, fast thoughts, interrupting, forgetting, and emotional surges may be nervous system clues.
- Beat 2: Support does not mean forcing stillness. It means making the task accessible.
- Beat 3: Try shorter directions, movement before sitting, visual steps, and immediate feedback.
- Beat 4: If your child is autistic and also seems driven by motion, novelty, or impulsive bursts, ask about ADHD support too.
- Close: "They are not too much. Their plan may be too thin."

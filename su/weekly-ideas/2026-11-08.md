## 1. Monday, November 9 | Educational

**Post type:** Carousel, 7 slides
**Hook:** “The hand dryer is off. The bathroom can still feel too loud.”

**Key points / slide outline:**
1. Cover with hook and a simple bathroom-door illustration.
2. List what we might overlook: echoes, flushing, bright lights, strong soap smells, unpredictable dryers.
3. Before entering, offer a simple preview of what is inside. Ask what support our child wants.
4. Look for a quieter restroom or quieter time when available. Do not promise silence.
5. Offer familiar hearing protection if our child wants it. Keep a preferred drying option available.
6. Agree on a way to request a pause or leave, using speech, gesture, or the child's communication system.
7. Saveable reminder: “The goal is access, not proving they can tolerate it.”

**CTA:** “Which bathroom detail do you wish more places would change?”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismParenting #SensoryAccessibility #AutismAcceptance #AccessibleSpaces #SpectrumUnlocked

## 2. Tuesday, November 10 | Emotional / relatable

**Post type:** Single graphic
**Hook:** “I miss being invited, even when we might say no.”

**Key points / caption outline:**
- “Sometimes we need the quieter plan, the shorter visit, or the option to leave.”
- “That does not mean we stopped wanting to belong.”
- Give friends a concrete invitation script: “We would love you there. What would make it easier? No pressure either way.”
- Close: “An invitation with room for our real life means more than you know.”
- Write as a community reflection, not an invented personal anecdote.

**CTA:** “Save this if you have been trying to find the words.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismFamily #InclusiveFriendships #ParentConnection #AutismCommunity #SpectrumUnlocked

## 3. Wednesday, November 11 | Engagement

**Post type:** Story, 4 frames
**Hook:** “The coat debate: which part needs a redesign?”

**Key points / interactive outline:**
1. Poll: “What gets in the way most?” Options: “Scratchy inside” / “Tricky fastening”.
2. Second poll: “Getting ready feels easier with...” Options: “A familiar layer” / “Two choices”. Both are possibilities, not prescriptions.
3. Question sticker: “What tiny clothing change made a difference in your house?” Ask for the feature, not a brand or purchase link.
4. Offer “Something else? Tell us.” Share anonymized replies later only with permission; describe them as family experiences, not universal solutions.

**CTA:** “Let's build a comfort-first clothing wish list.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #SensoryFriendly #AutismParents #EverydayAccessibility #AutismCommunity

## 4. Thursday, November 12 | Educational

**Post type:** Reel, 30 seconds, original voiceover and captions
**Hook:** “The package changed. To us, it is still the same snack. To our child, it may not be.”

**Key points / script outline:**
- 0-5s: Show two mock snack packages, no child distress footage.
- 5-12s: “A new wrapper can make something familiar feel uncertain. We do not need to argue that it is the same.”
- 12-20s: “Show the change ahead of time when possible. Let them look without pressure. Keep a familiar option available if you can.”
- 20-26s: “Do not secretly swap food or turn tasting into a test.”
- 26-30s: “We can explain the change without demanding an immediate yes.”

**CTA:** “Save for the next surprise packaging update.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismParenting #FoodPredictability #NeurodiversityAffirming #AutismAcceptance #SpectrumUnlocked

## 5. Friday, November 13 | Timely: World Kindness Day

**Post type:** Carousel, 6 slides
**Hook:** “World Kindness Day: kindness is letting a child say no to the hug.”

**Key points / slide outline:**
1. Cover: November 13, World Kindness Day.
2. Offer a wave, a spoken greeting, a communication-device greeting, or no greeting right now.
3. Ask before touching a child or their mobility/communication equipment.
4. Keep an invitation open without making eye contact or a hug the price of belonging.
5. Family script: “They do not owe us a hug. We are happy they are here.”
6. “This week, make one family gathering easier to join.” Save or share with a willing relative, not a public callout.

**CTA:** “What does respectful kindness look like in your family?”
**Suggested hashtags:** #WorldKindnessDay #AutismAcceptance #BodyAutonomy #InclusiveFamilies #SpectrumUnlocked

## 6. Saturday, November 14 | Emotional / relatable

**Post type:** Reel, 20 seconds, parent-only B-roll
**Hook:** “The family photo can be blurry. The memory still counts.”

**Key points / script outline:**
- 0-5s: Parent sets down a phone. Text: “We wanted one photo of everyone looking.”
- 5-12s: Film empty chairs, a shared activity, or a drawing instead. Voiceover: “We can remember the day without asking our child to perform it for the camera.”
- 12-17s: “Looking away, moving, or opting out does not make them less part of the family.”
- 17-20s: “Belonging does not need a perfect picture.”
- Do not stage coercion or use a child's vulnerable moment as the visual.

**CTA:** “Keep this reminder for holiday photo season.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismFamily #InclusiveParenting #FamilyPhotos #AutismAcceptance #SpectrumUnlocked

## 7. Sunday, November 15 | Engagement

**Post type:** Story, 4 frames
**Hook:** “You get to redesign one birthday-party tradition. What goes?”

**Key points / interactive outline:**
1. Poll: “Change one first...” Options: “Surprise singing” / “Everyone watching gifts”.
2. Poll: “An option you would add...” Options: “Quiet arrival slot” / “Come-and-go invitation”.
3. Question sticker: “What would your child keep, skip, or invent?” Preferences vary, including loving loud celebrations.
4. Closing: “There is no single autistic birthday party. The best plan leaves room for the person celebrating.” Invite participation from autistic adults as well as parents.

**CTA:** “Reply with one tradition you would rewrite. No names or private child details needed.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #InclusiveBirthdays #AutismCommunity #AutismAcceptance #NeurodivergentFamilies

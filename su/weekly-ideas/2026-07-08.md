## 1. Educational: Interoception, The Hidden Body Sense

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** “If your child melts down over ‘nothing,’ their body may have been shouting for an hour.”

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, “Autistic kids may miss body clues until they are urgent.”
2. Slide 2: Define interoception: the sense that helps us notice hunger, thirst, pain, temperature, bathroom needs, and emotions in the body.
3. Slide 3: What parents may see: sudden rage, panic, refusing tasks, bathroom accidents, “I do not know,” or crashing after seeming fine.
4. Slide 4: Why it matters: if the cue arrives late, the reaction can look bigger than the trigger.
5. Slide 5: Try body check-ins with visuals: hungry, thirsty, hot, cold, tired, pain, bathroom, need quiet.
6. Slide 6: Build routines instead of relying on cues: scheduled snacks, water, bathroom breaks, rest breaks.
7. Slide 7: Reframe: “They are not ignoring their body. Their body signals may be harder to read.”
8. Slide 8: CTA: “Save this for the next mystery meltdown.”

## 2. Educational: Summer Heat Can Look Like Behavior

**Post type:** Reel  
**Hook/headline:** “Before you call it defiance, check heat, sweat, thirst, and scratchy clothes.”

**Script outline:**
- “Summer can make regulation harder for autistic kids, not because they are being difficult, but because their nervous system is working overtime.”
- “Heat, humidity, sunscreen texture, bright sun, wet swimsuits, tags, bugs, and crowds can stack fast.”
- “Some kids do not notice thirst or overheating until they are already overloaded.”
- “Try a summer regulation kit: cold water, cooling towel, hat, backup clothes, safe snack, sunglasses, headphones, and a quiet exit plan.”
- “If behavior changes suddenly, ask: are they hot, thirsty, hungry, itchy, tired, or trapped?”
- “Comfort is not spoiling. Comfort is access.”

## 4. Emotional/Relatable: Sibling Love Can Be Complicated

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** “Siblings can love their autistic brother or sister and still need space.”

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, “Sibling feelings can be mixed, and that is normal.”
2. Slide 2: They may feel protective, proud, embarrassed, jealous, worried, or left out.
3. Slide 3: Mixed feelings do not mean they are unkind.
4. Slide 4: Try naming the truth: “Our family has different needs, and your needs matter too.”
5. Slide 5: Build small protected moments with the sibling, even 10 minutes counts.
6. Slide 6: Do not make them a mini therapist, interpreter, or backup parent.
7. Slide 7: Give scripts for public moments: “He is having a hard time. We are helping him.”
8. Slide 8: CTA: “What helps your kids feel seen as individuals?”

**Suggested hashtags:**

## 6. Engagement: Decode The Behavior

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** “What might this behavior be communicating?”

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Prompt, “Decode the behavior with us.”
2. Slide 2: “Child refuses shoes every morning.” Options: texture, transition, tightness, heat, demand avoidance, motor planning.
3. Slide 3: “Child screams when TV turns off.” Options: transition, loss of predictability, favorite interest interrupted, needs visual timer.
4. Slide 4: “Child runs from birthday party.” Options: noise, crowd, smell, lights, social pressure, need for movement.
5. Slide 5: “Child chews sleeves.” Options: oral sensory input, anxiety, concentration, boredom, regulation.
6. Slide 6: “Child says ‘I hate you’ after school.” Options: restraint collapse, hunger, exhaustion, safe-person release.
7. Slide 7: Comment prompt: “Pick one slide and tell us what you would check first.”

## 1. Monday, November 16 | Educational

**Post type:** Carousel, 7 slides
**Hook:** “The timer rang. But did we explain what happens next?”

**Key points / slide outline:**
1. Cover with timer and unfinished toy scene.
2. A timer announces an ending. It does not explain the next step.
3. Replace “Time's up!” with a concrete preview: “After this piece, we put the game here and go to the car.”
4. Offer a photo of the next place or a short written sequence, if helpful to our child.
5. Make a stopping place: take a picture of the build, save the game, or mark the page. Ask before moving their things.
6. Offer a realistic choice: “Carry it with us or leave it on this shelf?” Only offer options we can honor.
7. Close: “Make the next step clearer, not the countdown louder.” A timer can still help some children; this is an extra support, not a universal replacement.

**CTA:** “Save this for the activity that is hardest to leave.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismParenting #TransitionSupport #VisualSupports #NeurodiversityAffirming #SpectrumUnlocked

## 2. Tuesday, November 17 | Emotional / relatable

**Post type:** Single graphic
**Hook:** “I learned the names of 14 train stations. That was our conversation.”

**Key points / caption outline:**
- Frame as a suggested parent-perspective vignette, not a claim about the account owner's life.
- “Sometimes connection looks like learning the thing our child loves, instead of redirecting them to the thing we planned.”
- Mention drawing a route, listening to a favorite fact, or sitting nearby while they sort pictures.
- “We do not have to turn every shared interest into a lesson.”
- Close: “Their joy is worth getting to know.” Do not require eye contact or conversation in return.

**CTA:** “What topic has become part of your world because someone you love loves it?”
**Suggested hashtags:** #AutisticJoy #AutismFamily #SpecialInterests #ConnectionNotCompliance #SpectrumUnlocked

## 3. Wednesday, November 18 | Engagement

**Post type:** Story, 4 frames
**Hook:** “Design the waiting room with us. What changes first?”

**Key points / interactive outline:**
1. Poll: “One change first?” Options: “Turn off the TV” / “More space between seats”.
2. Poll: “An option you would use?” Options: “Wait outside + text” / “Quieter seating area”. These are wish-list features, not promises about providers.
3. Question sticker: “What would YOUR family add? Children and autistic adults welcome.”
4. Closing card: “Access can look different for each person.” Invite “Neither, we need...” replies. Re-share responses only with permission and without identifying details.

**CTA:** “Help us build a waiting room wish list, not a list of places to call out.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #SensoryAccessibility #AutismCommunity #AccessibleSpaces #AutismParents

## 4. Thursday, November 19 | Educational

**Post type:** Reel, 30 seconds, original voiceover with captions
**Hook:** “Before the holiday group chat, try a three-line host note.”

**Key points / script outline:**
- 0-5s: Parent-only B-roll typing an invitation reply: “We would love to come. Here is what helps us join.”
- 5-13s: Line one, logistics: “Could you tell us who will be there and roughly when food will be ready?”
- 13-21s: Line two, practical request: “Can we bring a familiar activity and use a quieter spot if needed?”
- 21-27s: Line three, flexibility: “We may make it a short visit. We will let you know when we head out.”
- 27-30s: “Share the support request, not your child's private history.” Put the editable three-line note in the caption.
- Adapt to the family's needs and host's space; no requirement to attend or explain a diagnosis.

**CTA:** “Save the script before the next invitation lands.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #InclusiveHolidays #AutismFamily #HolidayPlanning #AutismParenting #SpectrumUnlocked

## 5. Friday, November 20 | Timely: World Children's Day

**Post type:** Carousel, 6 slides
**Hook:** “World Children's Day: our children belong in the planning, not just the plan.”

**Key points / slide outline:**
1. Cover: November 20, World Children's Day.
2. Ask about one everyday decision: “What would you like to do together?”
3. Make participation accessible: pictures, objects, writing, speech, or the child's own communication system.
4. Offer real options and room for “neither” or “not now.” Do not treat a guessed preference as confirmed consent.
5. Show a simple example: choosing a shared activity and changing the plan in response to the child's choice.
6. Close: “Being included means our input can change something.” Invite families to make one decision together today.

**CTA:** “Which small decision could your child help shape this week?”
**Suggested hashtags:** #WorldChildrensDay #ChildrensVoices #AutismAcceptance #AccessibleCommunication #SpectrumUnlocked

## 6. Saturday, November 21 | Emotional / relatable

**Post type:** Reel, 20 seconds, adult-only B-roll
**Hook:** “You can repair a rushed moment without making your child comfort you.”

**Key points / script outline:**
- 0-5s: Adult places keys down and pauses. “We rushed. Our voice got sharp.”
- 5-12s: On-screen repair script: “I spoke too sharply. That was my responsibility. I am sorry.”
- 12-17s: “Next time, I will leave more time.” Name a realistic change, not a promise never to struggle.
- 17-20s: “An apology does not need a hug, a reply, or instant forgiveness.”
- Caption: “We can take responsibility without turning the moment into another job for our child.” Do not stage a child's distress.

**CTA:** “Save the words for a day when finding them feels hard.”
**Suggested hashtags:** #ParentingRepair #AutismParenting #ConnectedParenting #NeurodivergentFamilies #SpectrumUnlocked

## 1. Educational: Interoception - The Hidden Sense Behind Big Feelings

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** “Your child may not know they are hungry, thirsty, hot, or need the bathroom until it feels like an emergency.”

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, “The hidden sense autism parents need to know.”
2. Slide 2: Define interoception in parent-friendly language: sensing what is happening inside the body.
3. Slide 3: Why it matters: hunger, thirst, bathroom needs, pain, temperature, fatigue, and anxiety can show up as behavior.
4. Slide 4: What parents may see: sudden irritability, refusal, panic, aggression, shutdown, or “out of nowhere” meltdowns.
5. Slide 5: Try body check-ins with visuals: hungry, thirsty, tired, hot, bathroom, hurt, need break.
6. Slide 6: Build reminders into routines instead of waiting for your child to notice.
7. Slide 7: Reframe: “They are not ignoring their body. Their body signals may be hard to read.”
8. Slide 8: CTA: “Save this for the next ‘out of nowhere’ hard moment.”

## 2. Educational: The 10-Minute Transition Bridge

**Post type:** Reel  
**Hook/headline:** “If transitions are exploding your day, try a bridge instead of a countdown.”

**Script outline:**
- “A countdown tells your child something is ending. A bridge shows them how to get from here to there.”
- “Try this: 10 minutes before the transition, name what is ending, what is next, and what stays predictable.”
- “Example: ‘Tablet is ending soon. Then shoes, car, therapy. Your headphones come with us.’”
- “At 5 minutes, offer one regulated job: carry the keys, choose the shoes, hold the visual card.”
- “At 1 minute, reduce talking. Point to the visual and keep your body calm.”
- “After the transition, add recovery time if needed.”
- “The goal is not perfect compliance. The goal is less panic.”

## 7. Trending/Timely: Fireworks Week Sensory Prep

**Post type:** Reel or carousel  
**Hook/headline:** “Fireworks do not start on July 4 for sensory-sensitive kids. Prep starts now.”

**Key points/script outline:**
- “If your child is sound-sensitive, this is your reminder to prep before the neighborhood fireworks begin.”
- Check headphones now, not the night of.
- Make a low-sensory plan: inside room, white noise, favorite show, weighted blanket, comfort item.
- Preview the sounds with control: short video, low volume, stop whenever they ask.
- Plan language: “Loud sounds may happen. You are safe. We can go to the quiet room.”
- Tell relatives the plan before the event so you are not negotiating during overload.
- CTA: “Save this before fireworks week sneaks up on you.”

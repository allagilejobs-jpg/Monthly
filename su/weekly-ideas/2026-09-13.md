## 1. Educational

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** The folder that comes home empty is still communication

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, "No note does not always mean no problem."
2. Slide 2: Some children look fine at school because they are masking, freezing, or following routines on autopilot.
3. Slide 3: Empty folders can hide missed bathroom cues, lunch stress, peer confusion, noise overload, or shutdowns.
4. Slide 4: Ask for a 3-line daily check: food, regulation, social load.
5. Slide 5: Use neutral language: "What patterns did you notice today?" instead of "Did they behave?"
6. Slide 6: Keep one simple home log so you can compare school silence with home behavior.
7. Slide 7: CTA, "Save this before your next teacher message."

## 2. Educational

**Post type:** Reel  
**Hook/headline:** When your child cannot answer "How was school?", try this instead

**Script outline:**
1. Open with: "If your autistic child shuts down when you ask how school was, the question may be too big."
2. Explain: Open-ended questions require memory, sequencing, emotion labeling, and language access after a long day.
3. Replace with choices: "Was lunch loud or okay?" "Did your body feel tired or buzzy?" "Did anything feel unfair?"
4. Offer a no-talking option: thumbs up/down, color cards, emoji chart, drawing, or pointing.
5. Close with: "Connection first, details later."

**Suggested hashtags:** #AutismTips #AutismParent #CommunicationSupport #NeurodivergentKids #AfterSchoolRoutine #ParentingTools #AutismFamily #SpectrumUnlocked

## 4. Emotional/Relatable

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** The invisible cost of being "fine" at school

**Key points:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, "Fine at school can still cost a lot."
2. Slide 2: They answered, waited, sat, copied, smiled, complied, and held it together.
3. Slide 3: Then home gets the tears, silence, rigid demands, food refusal, or explosive reset.
4. Slide 4: This does not mean home is the problem.
5. Slide 5: It may mean home is where their body finally stops performing.
6. Slide 6: Try lower demands, predictable snacks, quiet time, and fewer questions after pickup.
7. Slide 7: CTA, "Comment FINE if you know this pattern."

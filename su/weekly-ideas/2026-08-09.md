## 1. Educational

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** "Make a one-page child guide before the teacher has to guess."

**Key points/script outline:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, "Your child's teacher does not need a novel. They need the right one page."
2. Slide 2: Add communication basics: how your child asks for help, says no, or shows overwhelm.
3. Slide 3: Add regulation clues: early signs before a meltdown, shutdown, or bolting risk.
4. Slide 4: Add supports that work: headphones, movement, visual timer, quiet space, first/then language.
5. Slide 5: Add supports that backfire: forced eye contact, surprise touch, public correction, too many questions.
6. Slide 6: Add motivators and comfort anchors: favorite topics, safe activities, preferred praise style.
7. Slide 7: Send it early and ask, "What format is easiest for your classroom team?"
8. Slide 8: CTA: "Comment GUIDE if you want a one-page teacher profile template."

## 2. Educational

**Post type:** Reel  
**Hook/headline:** "Lunch can be the loudest part of the school day."

**Key points/script outline:**
- "If your child comes home starving, lunch may not be about appetite."
- "Cafeterias can be loud, bright, rushed, crowded, unpredictable, and full of food smells."
- "Ask what lunch actually looks like: where they sit, how long they have, who helps, and what happens if they cannot eat."
- "Pack predictable foods, but also ask about quieter seating, extra time, or an adult check-in if needed."
- "A full lunchbox coming home is information, not a failure."
- CTA: "Save this before you assume picky eating is the whole story."

## 4. Emotional/Relatable

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** "If your child holds it together at school and falls apart at home, home is not the problem."

**Key points/script outline:**
1. Slide 1: Hook, "Home may be where the mask finally drops."
2. Slide 2: Some autistic children spend the whole school day coping, complying, and holding in stress.
3. Slide 3: The after-school crash can look like anger, tears, refusal, silence, or instant screen-seeking.
4. Slide 4: That does not mean they had a "perfect day." It may mean they worked hard all day.
5. Slide 5: Try fewer questions, safe food, dimmer lights, movement, or quiet before problem-solving.
6. Slide 6: You are not spoiling them by meeting the need underneath the crash.
7. Slide 7: CTA: "What does decompression look like in your house?"

## 6. Engagement

**Post type:** Carousel  
**Hook/headline:** "Bus, car line, or pickup: which transition needs the most support?"

**Key points/script outline:**
1. Slide 1: Prompt, "The school day does not start at the classroom door."
2. Slide 2: Bus transition: noise, waiting, seating, route changes, driver communication.
3. Slide 3: Car line transition: hurry, heat, long waits, separation, unpredictable timing.
4. Slide 4: Walker or daycare handoff: multiple adults, unclear scripts, fast goodbyes.
5. Slide 5: Pickup transition: hunger, fatigue, sensory overload, questions too soon.
6. Slide 6: Quick supports: visual sequence, goodbye script, comfort item, arrival plan, post-school quiet.
7. Slide 7: CTA: "Comment BUS, CAR LINE, PICKUP, or HANDOFF."

# Content Research - May 27, 2026

## 🔥 Trending Topics This Week

### 1. School safety and non-speaking child protection is the strongest news-adjacent lane
A Louisville/Kentucky story about parents using a hidden camera after concerns about mistreatment of their non-speaking autistic child is circulating across local news and viral/social sites. Disability Scoop's current popular list also includes related disability protection and education stories.

**Why it matters for SU:** Parents of minimally speaking and non-speaking children are highly alert to school safety, communication access, and how to know when something is wrong. This topic can become fear-heavy fast, so SU should avoid sensational retelling and turn it into practical, parent-grounded content.

**Content opportunities:**
- "If your child cannot tell you what happened at school, look for patterns"
- "School safety for non-speaking kids: what to document before there is a crisis"
- "A communication passport is not extra. It is protection."
- "What to ask the IEP team about supervision, reporting, and communication access"

### 2. Autism misinformation on social media is a timely trust-building topic
Disability Scoop highlighted a May 2026 study finding widespread autism and mental health misinformation on social platforms. This fits directly with parents searching TikTok, Instagram, Threads, and Facebook groups for fast answers.

**Why it matters for SU:** SU can become the calm, practical filter: not shaming parents for Googling at midnight, but teaching them how to spot red flags and choose safer sources.

**Content opportunities:**
- "3 autism advice red flags on social media"
- "If a post promises to cure autism, pause"
- "Before you try that viral meltdown tip, ask these 4 questions"
- "Social media can support you, but it should not replace your team"

### 3. PDA and demand avoidance are becoming a parent-search topic
A May 2026 IEPFocus guide frames PDA as an anxiety-driven autism profile, not defiance, and says standard compliance-based strategies can increase distress. This topic is also strongly aligned with parent pain points around school refusal, bedtime battles, transitions, and daily care demands.

**Why it matters for SU:** SU has covered meltdowns, masking, burnout, IEPs, and sensory supports. PDA lets us connect those lanes with a fresh frame: autonomy, anxiety, flexible demands, and collaborative language.

**Content opportunities:**
- "It may not be defiance. It may be demand panic."
- "PDA-friendly language swaps for everyday parenting"
- "When 'first, then' makes things worse"
- "Choice is not a reward. For some kids, it is regulation."

### 4. Summer sensory and travel access remains the seasonal anchor
Yesterday's research showed summer transition as the strongest immediate content lane. Today's Disability Scoop popular list also included autism training for airline staff, which reinforces travel, airport, vacation, and public-space access as a seasonal content cluster.

**Why it matters for SU:** Late May into June is when families start camps, flights, road trips, pools, cookouts, and irregular sleep schedules. Content should be highly practical and saveable.

**Content opportunities:**
- "The airport sensory plan: before, during, after"
- "Summer fun still needs recovery time"
- "The travel day sensory kit parents forget"
- "How to explain autism supports to family before vacation"

## 💡 Fresh Content Angles

### 1. "The Non-Speaking School Safety Checklist"
**Hook:** "If your child cannot tell you what happened, the system needs more ways to listen."

**Structure:** 5 slides: baseline behavior, communication passport, daily notes, body/sensory clues, IEP safety questions. Keep it empowering, not fear-based.

**Best format:** Carousel. Good Beacon tie-in if framed as "build your child's profile."

### 2. "Viral Autism Advice Red Flags"
**Hook:** "Not every autism tip on your feed deserves your trust."

**Structure:** Red flags: cure language, shame language, one-size-fits-all, ignores communication, recommends forcing eye contact/compliance, promises overnight results. End with: ask if it protects safety, dignity, communication, and regulation.

**Best format:** Reel or carousel. Strong share/save potential.

### 3. "PDA-Friendly Language Swaps"
**Hook:** "Sometimes the demand is the trigger, not the task."

**Structure:** Instead of "You need to..." try "Would it help if...?" Instead of "First this, then that" try "Do you want A or B first?" Instead of "Stop refusing" try "What feels too hard right now?" Include caveat: PDA is not a formal standalone diagnosis everywhere, but demand anxiety is real for many families.

**Best format:** Carousel with side-by-side scripts.

### 4. "Airport and Travel Sensory Plan"
**Hook:** "The airport is not one demand. It is twenty at once."

**Structure:** noise, lines, waiting, security, shoes/clothing, hunger, smells, delays, social pressure, recovery plan. End with one printable-style checklist.

**Best format:** Carousel or reel before summer travel.

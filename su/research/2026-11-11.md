## 1. Strongest timely topic: Thanksgiving without performance pressure

Multiple publishers are covering autistic children's holiday meals, unfamiliar environments, sensory access, and family expectations. This is the strongest seasonal publishing signal found this week. SU should prioritize a specific, usable planning tool rather than another generic list of tips.

Recent first-party publisher examples:
- [Admire ABA, November 7: Thanksgiving mealtime](https://www.admireabatherapy.com/blog/autism-thanksgiving-mealtime/): advance meal planning, familiar foods, early overload signals, and relatives' pressure. Competitor observation only, not an endorsement of its therapy model.
- [Autism Learning Partners, November 5: Thanksgiving participation](https://autismlearningpartners.com/thanksgiving-activities-for-children-with-autism/): kitchen/table activities and interest-led involvement.
- [The Autism Store, November 5: sensory-friendly Thanksgiving](https://www.autism-store.com/blogs/the-autism-blog/tagged/sensory-sensitivities): noise, unfamiliar food, and comfort.
- [Centria, November 2](https://centriahealthcare.com/resources/family-holiday-support-step-by-step-guide-to-calm-celebrations/): visual supports, family expectations, and holiday preparation. Earlier context, outside this week's window.

Recommended hook: “The holiday plan needs a leaving plan, not just an arrival time.”
Recommended asset: one-page holiday access card with familiar food, preferred communication, quiet space, an adult handoff, and a flexible leaving signal. No forced tasting, hugging, eye contact, or staying for the photo.

## 2. Other current signals

### School inclusion and bullying
[Autismo España published an updated school-bullying report November 5](https://autismo.org.es/actualidad/noticias/informe-acoso-escolar-2026/), highlighting exclusion and barriers to communicating mistreatment. This is a Spain-specific report, not a U.S. prevalence estimate; do not reuse its headline statistic as a universal fact.

Editorial implication: show how adults can provide accessible ways to report exclusion rather than telling children simply to speak up. A picture choice, AAC option, or trusted-adult check-in can be part of an individualized school discussion.

### Caregiver support that is concrete, not inspirational
[Caregiver Action Network's November 2026 campaign](https://www.caregiveraction.org/nfc-month/) uses the theme “Care Connects Us.” [ACL's November 1 notice](https://acl.gov/news-and-events/events-and-observances/nfcm) independently confirms the observance and theme. Adapt this to specific help with meals, transport, and appointments, without framing autistic children as burdens.

## 5. Fresh evergreen angles and recommended sequence

### Priority 1: Sensory plus Thanksgiving
Hook: “A quiet room only helps if our child can actually get to it.”
Format: 6-slide carousel and printable access card. Specify location, whether the route is clear, who can accompany the child, and a usable request signal. Ask the child what they prefer. Frame breaks as access, not a reward.
Tool fit: Sensory Profile Quiz only when it helps parents reflect on preferences; do not imply diagnosis or guaranteed prevention.

### Priority 2: IEP plus school return
Hook: “The break ends Monday. The support should not disappear Monday morning.”
Format: short email template asking the school team about a predictable arrival, familiar communication supports, and a check-in after a disruptive break. Document observable needs, not assumptions about the child's motives. This is a conversation starter, not legal advice or an assertion of automatic entitlement to a specific accommodation.
Freshness: extends existing IEP basics into a specific holiday-to-school transition.

### Priority 3: Therapy plus caregiver support
Hook: “Ask the therapist for one support we can keep, not ten holiday homework tasks.”
Format: Reel with three planning questions: What matters most to our child? What can stay familiar? What can wait? Invite families to discuss changes with their own care team; do not recommend stopping prescribed treatment.
Freshness: changes therapy-type explainers into a manageable holiday handoff. Connect to caregiver month through practical workload sharing.

Backup: “Telling an adult does not have to mean speaking.” An accessible school-reporting post for November 16-20. Use AAC/gesture/image options and adult accountability; no UK-targeted SU tool promotion.

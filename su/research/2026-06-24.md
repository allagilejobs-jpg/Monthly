## 1. Trending Topics, Hashtags, and Viral Formats

### Top trend for SU this week

**Stimming as self-care / regulation, especially in summer.**

Why it fits:
- TikTok search is surfacing “stimming meme” content with community tags like `#neurospicy`, `#autismmemes`, and `#stimming`.
- It is relatable, visual, and easy to turn into parent education.
- SU already has meltdown and sensory content, but not enough content that normalizes everyday regulation before crisis.

Content hooks:
- “That ‘weird’ thing your child does might be their nervous system helping them cope.”
- “Stimming is not the problem. The problem is asking a child to regulate silently.”
- “Summer break can increase stimming. Here’s why that is not automatically a regression.”

### Viral format adaptations

1. **“Oh well, whatever, nevermind” format**
   - Trend idea: one-line exhaustion/acceptance text over a calm or chaotic parent clip.
   - SU adaptation: “When the sensory plan works but the grandparents still call it spoiling.”
   - Best for Reels/TikTok humor with education in caption.

2. **“That’s My Why” three-slide format**
   - Trend idea: simple carousel showing motivation or emotional payoff.
   - SU adaptation:
     1. “Why I stopped saying ‘use your words’”
     2. “Because communication is bigger than speech”
     3. “That’s my why.”

3. **“Wow, ok” acting/read format**
   - SU adaptation: “Four ways people say ‘all kids do that’” with supportive, dismissive, sarcastic, and exhausted parent reads.
   - Use carefully, warm not mean.

4. **Glitch/freeze-frame highlight**
   - SU adaptation: freeze on the moment before a meltdown and label the hidden trigger: “lighting,” “transition,” “hunger,” “itchy seam,” “too many demands.”

## Notes for Voice and Safety

- Avoid framing stimming as a “symptom to reduce.” Frame as regulation, expression, or sensory input.
- Avoid making Disability Pride inspirational content about parents. Center dignity, access, and shame reduction.
- For elopement/water safety, be calm and practical, not fear-based.
- No em dashes in captions or graphics.

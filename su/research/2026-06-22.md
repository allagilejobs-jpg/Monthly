# Content Research - June 22, 2026

## 🔥 Trending Topics This Week

### 1. Summer childcare, family visits, and routine disruption are the strongest immediate lane
TikTok discovery results this week are surfacing autism-parenting clips around summer childcare, family visits, autistic children adjusting to change, parenting on the spectrum, and "family village" language. This lines up with the real-life June pain point: school structure is gone, camps and childcare are inconsistent, relatives may misunderstand support needs, and parents are trying to keep everyone regulated.

**Why it matters for SU:** SU has sensory hacks, meltdowns, IEPs, masking, burnout, ABA, and visual schedules, but the summer-content cluster needs more specific help around childcare handoffs, visiting relatives, camp/daycare expectations, and what to do when the village does not understand autism.

**Content opportunities:**
- "Before summer childcare starts, share this one-page autism profile"
- "Family visits are not a break if your child is masking the whole time"
- "The summer routine does not have to be rigid. It has to be readable."
- "If your village keeps saying 'they seem fine,' show them the recovery window"

### 2. Stimming humor and neurodivergent identity content are still discoverable
Current TikTok search snippets show stimming meme content using hashtags such as #neurospicy, #autismmemes, and #stimming. Humor and identity content can travel, especially when it validates everyday regulation rather than turning autistic traits into jokes for outsiders.

**Why it matters for SU:** This is a good way to make acceptance content lighter without becoming gimmicky. SU can use parent-friendly reframes: stimming is information, regulation, communication, and joy, not just something to stop in public.

**Content opportunities:**
- "What stimming can mean: joy, focus, stress, sensory input, communication"
- "Do not make 'quiet hands' the summer goal"
- "When relatives ask why your child does that movement"
- "Stimming is not the problem. Unsafe environments often are."

### 3. Autism misinformation remains a trust-building topic
Disability Scoop and other outlets continue to cite 2026 research showing widespread misleading autism and mental-health content on social platforms. One search result summarized that much online autism and mental health advice is misleading, while other coverage cited autism-related misinformation percentages on TikTok and Instagram.

**Why it matters for SU:** Parents are not wrong for searching social media, they are under-supported and often desperate for quick answers. SU can own the calm middle: validate the need for answers, then teach parents how to filter advice without shame.

**Content opportunities:**
- "Before you try that viral autism tip, ask these 4 questions"
- "Autism advice red flags: cure language, shame language, one-size-fits-all promises"
- "A post can feel relatable and still be unsafe advice"
- "Save this filter for late-night autism searches"

### 4. IEP and accommodations content is always-on, but competitors are going deeper into specifics
Autism Parenting Magazine search results this month highlight IEP goals, special education strategy, classroom accommodations, PDA and school struggles, assistive technology, sensory supports, breaks, visual schedules, and extra time. This is a competitor signal: broad IEP education is crowded, but practical scripts, measurable goals, and summer prep checklists still have room.

**Why it matters for SU:** SU has an IEP carousel, but needs more parent-ready micro-assets: what to ask for, how to word it, what data to collect over summer, and how to connect sensory/regulation supports to educational access.

**Content opportunities:**
- "Summer is IEP data season: what to track before school starts"
- "If the accommodation is only allowed after a meltdown, it is too late"
- "IEP goal examples parents can actually understand"
- "How to ask for sensory breaks without sounding vague"

## 💡 Fresh Content Angles

### 1. "The Summer Caregiver One-Pager"
**Hook:** "If someone is watching your autistic child this summer, do not rely on memory. Hand them a map."

**Structure:** 6 sections: communication, sensory triggers, calming supports, food/safety needs, transition warnings, what distress looks like before it becomes a meltdown.

**Best format:** Carousel plus downloadable/checklist CTA. Beacon tie-in: "Ask Beacon to help draft your child's one-page profile."

### 2. "The Relative Translation Guide"
**Hook:** "When family says 'they are fine,' they may only be seeing the mask."

**Structure:** Translate common comments:
- "They were fine with me" = they may collapse later
- "They need discipline" = they may need access and regulation
- "They will eat when hungry" = feeding can be sensory, motor, or anxiety-based
- "Everyone is tired" = autistic fatigue can be cumulative overload

**Best format:** Reel or carousel. Good share potential for family visits, cookouts, reunions, and summer holidays.

### 3. "Viral Autism Advice Filter"
**Hook:** "A viral autism tip should pass the dignity test before it enters your home."

**Structure:** Ask: Does it protect safety? Does it respect communication? Does it reduce fear? Does it avoid shame? Does it leave room for individual differences? Does it match what your child actually needs?

**Best format:** Carousel, saveable checklist, or short talking-head Reel.

### 4. "Disability Pride for Kids Who Need Support"
**Hook:** "Pride does not mean pretending support needs are easy. It means refusing shame around them."

**Structure:** Access is not special treatment, support is not failure, AAC is communication, breaks are participation tools, accommodations create opportunity.

**Best format:** July launch carousel. Keep tone grounded, not inspirational-poster.

## 🎯 Content Gaps to Fill

1. **Summer childcare and caregiver handoff tools** - SU has sensory and visual schedule content, but not a practical caregiver profile for camps, babysitters, grandparents, daycare, or summer programs.
2. **Family-boundary scripts for relatives** - Competitor/viral content touches family pressure. SU can make this practical and emotionally safe for parents before visits, reunions, and holidays.
3. **Stimming and public judgment** - SU should cover stimming as regulation and communication, especially with #stimming and #autismmemes discoverability.
4. **Misinformation literacy** - Still a gap with strong news relevance. SU can create recurring "safe advice filter" content.
5. **IEP prep during summer** - Competitors cover IEP goals and accommodations. SU should own the bridge between summer observations and August school planning.
6. **Disability Pride Month content** - July needs an affirming, parent-friendly campaign around dignity, access, accommodations, and support needs.

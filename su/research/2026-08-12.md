# Spectrum Unlocked Content Research
**Date:** 2026-08-12 (Wednesday)
**Focus:** Autism parenting niche trends, awareness dates, content gaps, and fresh evergreen angles

## 1) What is trending this week

### Top trending topic: Back-to-school discipline, accommodations, and "bad kid" labeling
The strongest fresh hook today is school discipline for autistic and ADHD children. A Georgia story published today described a 6-year-old autistic child with ADHD who was suspended after biting a sandwich into the shape of a gun, with his parents saying repeated disciplinary action came before appropriate accommodations were in place.

Why this matters for SU:
- It is local-ish to the Atlanta/Georgia audience and emotionally sticky for parents.
- It connects directly to August back-to-school anxiety, IEP access, behavior plans, and punitive school responses.
- The parent pain point is not abstract: "My child is being treated like a bad kid before anyone understands the disability."

SU angle:
- "Before the school labels it behavior, ask what support was missing."
- "The discipline question parents can ask before the first referral."
- "What should be in the plan before suspension becomes the pattern?"

### Continuing trend: Back-to-school transition prep
Back-to-school autism content is still dominating current provider and creator posts. The most repeated guidance is routines, visual schedules, teacher introductions, classroom visits, sensory kits, and IEP/504 review. This topic is crowded, so SU should go more specific than broad transition tips.

The most useful subtopics right now:
- Teacher handoff one-pagers
- Bus, car line, lunchroom, hallway, and recess transition plans
- After-school restraint collapse
- First-week IEP/504 implementation checks
- Parent anxiety after drop-off

### Secondary trend: After-school restraint collapse
Restraint collapse is getting renewed attention because families are moving from summer routines into school-day masking, compliance, noise, transitions, and fatigue. Understood and Undivided both frame it as the release that can happen when a child holds it together at school and melts down once safely home.

SU angle:
- "Home is not the problem. Home is where the nervous system finally exhales."
- "The first 20 minutes after pickup are not for questions."
- Tie to the Meltdown Tracker: track time of day, food, bus/lunch/recess notes, sleep, and teacher comments.

### Secondary trend: Sensory needs as school self-advocacy
A new 2026 sensory guide published this week emphasizes the eight senses, including vestibular, proprioception, and interoception. Competitors are shifting from generic sensory-product lists toward helping children name triggers, communicate needs, and reflect after school.

SU angle:
- "The 8-sense school check-in your child can point to."
- "A sensory need is not a preference if it protects access to learning."
- "What to tell the teacher when your child cannot explain the trigger yet."

## 3) Content gaps

Context checked: SU content hub already has 15 ready day folders covering myths, acceptance, IEP accommodations, masking, ABA, visual supports, meltdowns, sensory hacks, caregiver self-care, and related evergreen topics. Recent weekly ideas also cover back-to-school clothing battles, first-week demand reduction, access plan check-ins, teacher one-pagers, lunch sensory support, and after-school crash.

### Gap A: Discipline before support
Competitors have plenty of back-to-school checklists, but fewer posts help parents respond when a child is being disciplined for disability-related behavior.

Opportunity:
- Carousel: "Before the discipline referral, ask these 5 support questions"
- Reel hook: "Your child does not need 15 suspensions before the school tries support"
- Include: IEP/504 request, FBA/BIP language, documentation log, calm email script, behavior-as-communication framing

### Gap B: First disciplinary incident email script
Parents often freeze after the first phone call from school. SU can provide scripts without sounding legalistic.

Opportunity:
- Post: "The email to send after the school says your autistic child was 'unsafe'"
- CTA: "Comment SCRIPT for the school incident email template"

### Gap C: Re-entry plan after suspension or school refusal
Most content stops at prevention. The gap is what happens after a hard day, suspension, elopement, refusal, or a teacher call.

Opportunity:
- Carousel: "Do not send them back with no re-entry plan"
- Include: safe adult, sensory reset location, reduced demands, repair script, home-school check-in, data review

### Gap D: Child self-esteem after repeated correction
The Georgia story makes the emotional consequence clear: some kids begin to believe they are "bad." SU can speak to the parent side of rebuilding identity after repeated school discipline.

Opportunity:
- Reel: "When school makes your child feel like the problem"
- Include: strengths language, repair at home, teacher language requests, and separating child identity from behavior

### Gap E: Interoception at school
SU has touched interoception in weekly ideas, but not a school-specific carousel. This is a fresh way into bathroom accidents, hunger crashes, overheating, pain, and emotional awareness.

Opportunity:
- Carousel: "Your child's behavior may be an interoception clue"
- Include: nurse visits, bathroom plan, snack/hydration, temperature, movement breaks, pain reports

## 4) Fresh angles on evergreen topics

### IEP and 504
- **Angle:** "The IEP is not implemented until the adults know the plan."
- Content idea: first-week implementation checklist for teacher, specials, lunch, bus, substitute, recess, and front office.

### School discipline
- **Angle:** "A behavior plan should teach skills, not just count incidents."
- Content idea: parent-friendly explainer of FBA/BIP, what data to ask for, and what support should change after repeated referrals.

### Sensory
- **Angle:** "Make sensory supports portable across the school day."
- Content idea: classroom kit, lunch kit, bus/car kit, after-school reset kit, with the child's communication method included in each.

### Therapy
- **Angle:** "Ask the therapist what the school needs to know."
- Content idea: OT/SLP/PT back-to-school handoff questions, including sensory triggers, AAC access, movement needs, and communication repair.

### Parenting
- **Angle:** "Your first school call does not have to become a shame spiral."
- Content idea: three-part response: regulate yourself, ask for facts, request support review.

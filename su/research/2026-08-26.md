The current content opportunity is back-to-school for autistic and neurodivergent kids, especially the messy middle after day one: day 3/day 10 check-ins, IEP updates before routines harden, sensory self-advocacy language, and parent-school communication scripts.

## 4. Fresh Content Angles

### Angle 1: The day 3/day 10 back-to-school check-in
Carousel idea:
1. "The first day is not the test."
2. Day 3: what was harder than expected?
3. Day 10: which routines are actually holding?
4. What to ask the teacher.
5. What to document for the IEP.
6. CTA: "Comment SCHOOL for the check-in list."

Why it works: it uses current back-to-school interest, but moves beyond generic preparation.

### Angle 2: IEP goals vs accommodations, in parent language
Carousel idea:
1. "An IEP goal is not the same as an accommodation."
2. Goal: skill being taught.
3. Accommodation: access being provided.
4. Red flag: goal asks your child to mask.
5. Better wording examples.
6. CTA: "Save before your next meeting."

Why it works: competitors are covering IEPs, but parents need simple distinctions they can use in meetings.

### Angle 3: Teach sensory language before overload
Carousel idea:
1. "Do not wait for a meltdown to talk about sensory needs."
2. What does too loud feel like?
3. What does too bright feel like?
4. What helps my body feel ready?
5. Teacher note template.
6. CTA: "Comment SENSORY for the school note."

Why it works: it connects sensory, school, communication, and meltdown prevention without repeating old sensory hack content.

### Angle 4: Parent input that belongs in the IEP
Short-form video idea:
- Hook: "If your parent input says 'he struggles with transitions,' it is too vague."
- Teach a formula: trigger + observable sign + support that helps + what happens without support.
- Example: "After loud lunch, he covers ears and hides under the table. Headphones and a quiet 5-minute reset help him return to class."

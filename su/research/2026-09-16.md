# Spectrum Unlocked Content Research - 2026-09-16

Run time: Wednesday, September 16, 2026, 12:00 PM America/New_York

## Executive Summary

The best content opportunity this week is after-school restraint collapse and school refusal. Back-to-school content has moved from preparation into "why is my child falling apart after school?" Parents are seeing the pattern now: teacher says the day was fine, then the child melts down, shuts down, refuses homework, or starts resisting school the next morning.

Recommended SU positioning: help parents translate the afternoon crash into useful pattern tracking, teacher language, and IEP/504 accommodations. This is a natural bridge into October Sensory Processing Awareness Month, ADHD Awareness Month, AAC Awareness Month, and inclusion/access content.

## 1. Trending Topics, Hashtags, and Viral Signals

### After-school restraint collapse is the top trend

- Fresh August/September parenting and autism content is naming after-school restraint collapse as a common pattern, especially for autistic children who mask and tolerate sensory demands all day.
- The parent pain point is immediate and emotionally loaded: "They were fine at school, then fell apart the second they got home."
- SU should treat this as nervous-system overload, not bad behavior. Strongest format: "The first 30 minutes after school" carousel or short video.
- Source: Admire ABA, "After-School Restraint Collapse: Why Kids Melt Down After School," published August 16, 2026. https://www.admireabatherapy.com/blog/after-school-restraint-collapse/
- Source: Parents.com, "6 Ways To Help Your Kid Get Through 'Restraint Collapse' After School," published August 2026. https://www.parents.com/6-ways-to-help-your-kid-get-through-restraint-collapse-12030425

### School refusal is becoming the next parent panic topic

- Search results and competitor posts are focusing on autistic school refusal, especially anxiety, sensory overload, morning distress, bullying/social pressure, executive functioning, and routine shifts.
- The useful SU angle is "won't go" vs "can't go." This turns blame into problem-solving and fits SU's warm parent voice.
- Best hook: "If school mornings feel impossible, this may not be defiance."
- Source: Lighthouse Autism Center, "School Refusal & Autism," published April 23, 2025. https://lighthouseautismcenter.com/blog/autistic-child-school-refusal/
- Source: Autism Association of Australia, "Autism and school refusal," crawled September 2026. https://www.autismaustralia.org.au/navigating-autism/autism-and-school-refusal

## 4. Fresh Content Angles

### Angle 1: The first 30 minutes after school

Carousel:

1. "The school day isn't over when the bell rings."
2. Why restraint collapse happens after masking all day.
3. Do first: snack, water, quiet, low light, no questions.
4. Wait on homework, chores, and "how was your day?"
5. Track: day, transition, food, sensory load, recovery time.
6. CTA: "Comment AFTER SCHOOL for the decompression checklist."

### Angle 2: They are fine at school, not fine at home

Short-form:

- Hook: "If the teacher says 'we don't see that here,' try this."
- Explain masking and safe-place collapse.
- Give a script: "At school, they may be holding it together. At home, we see the cost. Can we look at supports that reduce the load before dismissal?"
- CTA: "Save this before your next school email."

### Angle 3: School refusal is a signal

Carousel:

1. "School refusal is not always refusal."
2. It may be anxiety, sensory pain, bullying, transitions, or burnout.
3. Track when it starts: Sunday night, morning, parking lot, classroom door.
4. Ask for a meeting before it becomes chronic absence.
5. Supports to request: gradual entry, safe person, sensory breaks, modified arrival, check-in/check-out.
6. CTA: "Comment SCHOOL for the question list."

### Angle 4: Sensory support is not just headphones

Carousel for October warm-up:

1. "Sensory support is not just headphones."
2. Cafeteria plan.
3. Bathroom plan.
4. Fire drill plan.
5. Bus/arrival/dismissal plan.
6. Assembly/recess/PE plan.
7. CTA: "Save this for your IEP notes."

# Content Research - May 26, 2026

## 🔥 Trending Topics This Week

### 1. Summer Transition + Sensory Recovery Is the strongest immediate content lane
School is ending, Memorial Day gatherings just happened, and parents are moving into camps, pools, cookouts, travel, different sleep schedules, sunscreen battles, and disrupted routines. Recent search results and competitor posts keep tying autism support to sensory flooding, meltdowns, movement needs, and classroom/home transitions.

**Why it matters for SU:** This is timely, practical, and highly shareable. Parents are not just asking "how do I make summer fun?" They are asking how to survive a season with less structure, more heat, more noise, more social expectations, and more unexpected changes.

**Content opportunities:**
- "Fun can still overload your child"
- "School ended, but your child's nervous system still needs a schedule"
- "The summer outing exit plan every autism family needs"
- "Heat, sunscreen, swimsuits, noise: the summer sensory stack"

### 2. IEP goal content is heating up again before summer reviews and fall planning
Competitor content in May 2026 includes detailed IEP goal guides, SMART goal explainers, self-regulation goals, behavior goals, communication goals, and classroom de-escalation strategies.

**Why it matters for SU:** SU has covered IEP basics, written requests, and advocacy, but competitors are leaning into specific goal examples. We can own a warmer, parent-friendly angle: goals that match real-life regulation, communication, and access needs, not just compliance.

**Content opportunities:**
- "If your IEP goal says 'reduce meltdowns,' ask this instead"
- "IEP goals should teach access, not just obedience"
- "3 self-regulation goals that actually support autistic kids"
- "What to ask for before school starts back"

### 3. Neurodiversity language is still a discovery topic for educators and parents
A May 2026 IEPFocus guide frames neurodiversity for teachers and parents, including IEPs, 504s, classroom support, home support, and myths. This suggests ongoing search interest around "what does neurodiversity actually mean?"

**Why it matters for SU:** Parents may hear the word everywhere but still need practical translations. SU can bridge the gap between affirming language and daily support: sensory access, communication access, and dignity during hard moments.

**Content opportunities:**
- "Neurodiversity is not a buzzword. It changes how we parent."
- "Affirming does not mean permissive. It means accurate."
- "What neurodiversity looks like at home, not just in captions"

### 4. New CDC prevalence numbers remain a data-backed hook
CDC's latest ADDM data reports about 1 in 31 children aged 8 has been identified with autism. It also notes autism is reported across racial, ethnic, and socioeconomic groups, and is over 3 times more common among boys than girls.

**Why it matters for SU:** This can be used carefully as context, not fear content. Pair the stat with support access, earlier identification, better school planning, and the need for parent-friendly tools.

**Content opportunities:**
- "1 in 31 is not just a statistic. It is classrooms, playgrounds, churches, and families."
- "More identified children means more families need practical support"
- "What the autism numbers do and do not tell us"

## 💡 Fresh Content Angles

### 1. "The Summer Sensory Stack"
**Hook:** "It may not be the pool. It may be the stack."

**Structure:** Heat + sunscreen smell + wet clothes + crowd noise + hunger + schedule change + surprise attention = overload. End with a practical reset: one sensory anchor, one exit plan, one recovery block.

**Format:** Carousel or reel. Highly saveable before pools, camps, cookouts, church events, and family travel.

### 2. "IEP Goals That Support Access, Not Compliance"
**Hook:** "If the goal is only 'stop the behavior,' it may miss what your child needs."

**Structure:** Reframe behavior goals into access goals. Examples: ask for break, use AAC/gesture, identify sensory discomfort, request help, transition with visual support, recover after overwhelm.

**Format:** Carousel with before/after wording.

### 3. "Autistic Pride Day Prep: What Parents Can Do Before June 18"
**Hook:** "Autistic Pride Day is not about parents speaking louder. It is about autistic people being respected."

**Structure:** Follow autistic creators, avoid puzzle imagery, use identity language respectfully, ask what pride and comfort mean for your child, teach siblings dignity language, share supports not savior stories.

**Format:** Reel or carousel. Good bridge from parent account to autistic-led perspective.

### 4. "Global Day of Parents: The invisible checklist"
**Hook:** "Some parents are carrying a whole care plan in their head."

**Structure:** Appointments, forms, sensory bag, food planning, school emails, behavior decoding, clothing backups, recovery time, insurance calls, therapy notes. Validate without martyr framing.

**Format:** Single graphic or reel for June 1.
